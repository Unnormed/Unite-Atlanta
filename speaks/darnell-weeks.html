The Biggest Show of the Year Happened Ten Minutes From My House

Darnell Weeks
Producer · East Atlanta

I have lived in East Atlanta my entire career. I have made records in basements on Moreland and mixed songs you know by heart in rooms with no sign on the door. So explain this to me: the biggest show of the year happened ten minutes from my house, and I read about it the next morning like a tourist.

This is not a complaint about my own FOMO. It's about what that missed night costs the city. Every show that half of us miss is a room where a connection didn't happen, a young engineer who didn't get pulled up on stage, a deal that didn't start over a drink afterwards. Atlanta's magic has always been proximity: everybody is here. But proximity without information is just geography.

We keep saying the scene is a family. Families tell each other where dinner is. Right now we operate like distant cousins who find out about the reunion through somebody's story post, after the plates are cleaned.

One calendar fixes a stupid amount of this. Not an algorithm deciding what I should care about. A plain, honest list, kept by us, of what is happening in this city. I'll take that over another think piece about Atlanta's influence. We know we're influential. I'd like to know what's happening on Thursday.
