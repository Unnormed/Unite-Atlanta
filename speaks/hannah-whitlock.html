Recap: What I Heard at the First First Wednesday

Hannah Whitlock
Talent Booker · Decatur

I went to the first First Wednesday expecting a mixer with a topic taped on top. I have been to a lot of those. This was different in the first ten minutes, because the first ten minutes were quiet: one question on the screen, what does this election cycle mean for the people who make music here, and a room that actually stopped to think about it.

Three things I wrote down. One: nobody in the room could name who at City Hall is responsible for music. Not the label people, not the venue people, not me. Two: half the scheduling fights in this town are accidents. Two organizers found out, standing next to each other, that they had booked the same Saturday for the same audience. They moved one date before the wine came out. Three: the under-thirties and the over-fifties want the same thing, which is to be told what is going on before it has already happened.

What didn't work: the room was heavy on executives and light on working musicians, and a seven o'clock start on a weeknight is part of why. If these are meant to be the city's rooms, the players have to be able to get there after soundcheck. I said so to the organizers and they wrote it down, which is more than I usually get.

I have booked rooms in this town for twenty-six years and I met more people I should already know in one evening than in the last five. I'll be at the next one. If you were there and heard it differently, write your own recap. That is apparently something we can do now.
