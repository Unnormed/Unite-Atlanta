There Is More Than One Atlanta Music Scene. They Have Never Met.

Camila Reyes
Promoter · Buford Highway

I book shows on Buford Highway. Regional Mexican, reggaeton, Latin indie, sometimes all three in one night. Most weekends I sell out a thousand-cap room. Most weekends, people who have worked in Atlanta music for twenty years have no idea it happened.

I don't say that to complain. I say it because the reverse is also true. I have missed panels, showcases and deals in Midtown and on the Westside that would have changed my year, because nobody in my world was on the thread where they got announced. We are not ignoring each other. We just have no place where we would ever see each other.

Atlanta talks about its music scene in the singular. From where I stand there are at least six: hip-hop and R&B, gospel, country and Americana, Latin, the indie rooms, the Korean and Vietnamese shows up in Gwinnett. Each one has its own promoters, its own flyers, its own group chats. Each one thinks it is the whole city.

A shared calendar will not merge those scenes, and it shouldn't. What it does is put them on the same page, literally. The first time a sound engineer from East Point sees my Friday and I see hers, something starts. I submitted my October shows the day this opened. Go look. Then put yours next to them.
