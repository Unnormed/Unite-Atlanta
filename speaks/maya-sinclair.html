Festival Season Without the Festival

Maya Sinclair
Student Journalist · Clark Atlanta

Every October this city has a heartbeat you can set your watch to. Hotels fill, group chats light up, and for one weekend the whole industry remembers that Atlanta is not a market, it is the market. This year, that weekend is just gone. A short statement in the local paper, and then nothing.

I have spent three weeks asking people who should know what happened. Promoters point at the city. The city points at costs. Costs point at insurance, insurance points at everything. What nobody has done is stand in front of the community that built its fall around this festival and explain it plainly.

Here is why that silence matters more than the cancellation. Festivals come and go; that is the business. But a scene that finds out about its own losses from a press release learns to expect nothing from the people in charge. Students like me, trying to build careers here, read that silence as a lesson: you are on your own.

The October First Wednesdays session is putting the right people in one room to take questions. I will be there with a notebook and exactly one question: if the festival could not be saved, who was ever going to tell us?
