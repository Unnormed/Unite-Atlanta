A $300 Million Deal Closed in This City. Nobody Told the City.

Renée Holloway
Label Executive · Buckhead

When a company built on Atlanta artists, Atlanta producers and Atlanta taste sells for three hundred million dollars, that is not just business news. That is civic news. That deal touches session rates, publishing splits, studio bookings, who signs next and who gets dropped. And the community that generated that value found out from a trade headline written in New York.

I have sat in the rooms where these deals happen. I understand confidentiality, and I understand why announcements are choreographed. But there is a difference between confidentiality before a deal and silence after one. After the ink dries, somebody owes this city a conversation: here is what we sold, here is what stays, here is what it means for the people who work here.

Other industries do this. When a plant changes owners, the workers get a town hall. Our plant is a network of studios, stages and kitchens, and our workers are engineers, dancers, songwriters and drivers. They deserve better than finding out their ecosystem changed hands from a push notification.

So here is my ask, to my own side of the table: when the next deal closes, and it will, come address the room. Unite Atlanta is building that room on purpose. Use it. The city that made you rich is owed at least an explanation.
