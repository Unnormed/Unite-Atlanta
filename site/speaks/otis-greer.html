Atlanta Speaks AUG 7, 2026

Ask Them Now, While Asking Is Still Possible

Otis Greer
Archivist · Westview

I collect tapes. DAT reels from studios that no longer exist, flyers from clubs that are condos now, radio rips with the DJ talking over the intro, which used to annoy me and now feels like the most valuable part. The music survives. The context is what dies.

Here is what keeps me up at night: the people who built this sound are still here. They shop at the same Publix as you. Some of them will talk your ear off for the price of a coffee. Every year we wait, a few more of those conversations become impossible, and no reissue, no documentary, no museum wall text can reconstruct what a first-hand voice carries.

Atlanta is world-class at making history and careless about keeping it. We let other cities write our story in their magazines, with their framing, on their schedule. Then we complain about the framing.

The fix is not complicated and it is not expensive. It's a calendar entry: sit a legend in a chair once a month, press record, ask good questions, keep the file safe. Legacy Night is that chair. If you knew this scene in the eighties and nineties, your memory is the archive. And if you're twenty-two and think history starts with your favorite artist's first mixtape, come find out whose shoulders that mixtape stands on. Ask them now. While asking is still possible.
